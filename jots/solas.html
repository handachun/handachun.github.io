The Five Solas

What Makes a Christian?

The Five Solas (or solae) are Sola Gratia (Grace Alone), Sola Fide (Faith Alone), Solus Christus (Christ Alone), Sola Scriptura (Scriptures Alone), and Soli Deo Gloria (Glory to God Alone). Though these five solas are most popularly associated with the theology of the reformers which freed the Church from errors of the Roman Catholic church, they are also quite fitting in explaining the basic tenets of Christianity. A Christian is a Christian because he is one who has been saved by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone, as revealed through the Scriptures alone, and to the glory of God alone.

In this short essay, I hope to introduce these five solas and to show how each of the solas distinguish a Christian from the rest of the world.


Sola Gratia

The Christian is saved by grace alone. That is, the Christian is saved, not by their fulfillment of works or by their ability to abide by the law as given by God, but by the unadulterated, selfless love of God that bestows upon us His Grace — the unmerited, undeserved gift of salvation. The Christian religion, and only the Christian religion, requires the utter dependence of its followers to God’s grace and goodness. It is not the “good works” of an individual, or the collective “good will” of a community that determines the trajectory of the eternal destination of mankind, but merely, the sovereignty of God. As God had produced the cosmos out of the divine benevolence of His heart and out of His sovereign will, so will His creation also become redeemed from their own fallen nature.

Mankind had fallen due to his abject disobedience to God in His sins, yet God, in His mercy, offered grace that would allow the recipients of such a gift to become adopted into His family. God need not have humbled Himself to the point of saving mankind, but He did so out of His own desire to do so. Unlike the gods of the Greeks who could be swayed by gifts offered by inquiring humans who sought to acquire favor from the gods, the God of the Bible does not desire gifts from sinful humans, but rather seeks to give the ultimate gift of salvation to those elect who He has chosen to be His children.

Unlike the Islamic religion, or the pantheistic Hindu religion, the Christian religion founds itself on not the works of man that can rise to please the gods, but rather founds itself on the firm foundation of the works of God that pleases God enough to cover our own crimes. No amount of human works will make God to “owe” us salvation, or blessing, or anything, but rather our ownership of the grace freely given will be the determiner of our eternal fate. The Christian is also unlike those of self-serving philosophies such as the Buddhist who strives in avoiding suffering by removing desires or living morally, as such an action would merely be equivalent of any theistic religion (though Buddhism claims not to be one) with the god being the self — the individual works to please the one god of the self, resulting in futility.


Sola Fide

If we are saved by the grace of God alone, we are recipients of such gifts through faith alone. Now, I want to be clear that it is not that we have faith prior to our gift of grace, but rather that by grace we have faith, and by faith that grace is ours. If anything, grace includes our faith which can only be instantiated if we are regenerate individuals through the grace of God. These five solas are not chronological directions to salvation, but the set of exclusivist principles that separate the Christian religion from the secular.

“Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen.” (Hebrews 11:1). Though the Christian has not seen Christ in heaven preparing a room for His believers, we believe that there is the absolving of our sins and a putting on of righteousness in the blood of our savior. Though the Christian cannot hold onto the fabric of Christ’s righteousness on this earth, at this moment, they hope and are assured that the object of their faith, Jesus, will be one day with them. Though the Christian may daily struggle with the battle against sin and maybe often feel rattlings of deathly chains that remind him of his works from days before, it is with faith that they march on as soldiers for Christ, knowing that their sins have been forgiven.

The Christian faith says that God is enough. Because God’s grace was enough, the Christian may have faith to know that they are justified by the works of God and not their own works — they are justified by faith. To add human works to the grace of God offered to man that was received in faith would be to discredit the completeness of God’s salvation and would be evidence that they do not have faith to begin with.


Solus Christus

The Christian is a Christian because they have been saved by grace through faith in Christ alone. This is the doctrine of justification by faith. In the center of every narrative, poetry, prophecy, and letter in the Bible, there is Christ. The most distinguishing factor of Chrst-ianity from any other religion is the very man and God Christ. He is the only truly man, truly God who not only lived a perfect life, but died and was risen again, and is waiting for His saints. He is the grace given to us, and He is the object of a Christian’s faith.

Without Christ, there is no Christianity. Christianity is not an amalgamation of sentimental songs, altruistic intents and works, a set of comforting stories, and fearmongering. No, Christianity is the story of Christ — He is the main character of the universe, and He is the perfecter of our faith. It was through Christ that all things were made that were made. It was Christ who was the hope of the nation of Israel in all its history. It was Christ Jesus who became flesh. It was Christ Jesus who lived a sinless, perfect, righteous life. It was Christ who was condemned to die a death of a sinner that was undeserving of His perfection. It was Christ Jesus who was buried but risen again on the third day. It was Christ Jesus who has ascended into heaven. It is Christ Jesus who rules as the head of the Church today. It is Christ Jesus who will come again to judge the living and the dead. Christ Jesus was and is and will be the center of the Christian faith.

Any religion that does not contain this Jesus of Nazareth who was the Christ, the Messiah, is a religion that is false to the core of its existence. Any heretical organization that puts this central figure of Christ into the background, or makes Him to be secondary, or tertiary, or any less prominent than primary to the doctrine, are as false as those that do not regard Christ at all. The Jesus of the Christian faith is God — any religion that says otherwise has no god. The Jesus of the Christian faith became man — any religion that says otherwise has no salvation for only man can pay for man’s sin. The Jesus of the Nazareth is the only Messiah — any religion that says otherwise is hopeless. The Jesus of the Christian faith is living — any religion that says otherwise is dead.

The Church is in Christ because in Him we are considered righteous as He took upon Himself the sin of mankind. The Bride has faith in Christ alone because they are in everlasting covenant with Him.


Sola Scriptura

So, how does a Christian know that they must be saved by grace through faith in Christ?

Such magnificence is revealed to us through the very written Word of God — the Scriptures, the Bible. If the Christian faith is predicated on God, then all its wisdom and truths must be given by the same God, the primary source. All assured knowledge and working of God are hence given to the Christian through the Bible, and its contents are sufficient in the provision of the necessary doctrinal truths that testify of a proper Christian faith.

Only the sixty-six books of the Bible can be accepted as the verified, written Word of God. This is not to say that the Christian may not understand those facts of God that are not directly illustrated within the pages of Scripture, but such theology must never circumvent, defy, or be ignorant of limits set by the Word of God. Therefore, any other claims to extra-Biblical “revelations” that contradict or shift the revealed truths from God must be displaced from our minds as they are creations of the human mind. Any “spiritual experiences” that command an instruction that separates from the original teachings of the Bible are to be considered a heresy. Any “interpretation” that does not hold in unity the fullness of the Bible, hence ascribing to God a sense of mutability, is to be thought of as the secularization of a sacred religion.

The Bible is above the Church as Christ is above the Church. The commands of the master is always of authority over the servants, and therefore, the Word of God is always over the Christians. The Christian faithful recognizes the fallen nature of the human heart and therefore relies not on the emotional sentiment and preference over the given Word of God. To commit such an act of placing the desires of the human heart over the Word of God would be to usurp the authority of the Lord. If there ever is a contest between the Scriptures and the human sense of right and wrong, the Scriptures must always be embraced for man changes, but God and His Word is eternal.

Only the Christian Bible is the sacred Word of God. The Qur’an, for example, contends that it is the unadulterated words of Allah that supersedes the Scriptures that came before it. However, the Qur’an would be arguing against itself if it considers the Scriptures viable for consumption at all, for the Bible teaches a God who is far different from the Allah of the Qur’an. If God is constant as the Bible claims, the altered Allah of the Qur’an is but a human creation. The Mormons suffer from the same ailment, but with a different name.


Soli Deo Gloria

What for? Why must we know through the Scriptures that we are saved by grace through faith in Christ?

For the Glory of God alone! There is no greater meaning to a man’s life than to live for God as the man has been created for such a purpose. From the beginning of the salvation economy to the end, the purpose of all things was to give glory to God. God created the heavens and the earth for His glory. God formed man out of the dust and breathed life for His glory. God permitted man to sin against God for His glory. God planned the salvation of mankind even before the Fall for His glory. God raised up the nation of Israel for His glory. God made His covenants with the disobedient descendents of Abraham for His glory. God sent His Son for His glory. God saw it fit to crush the Son for His glory. Jesus was raised on the third day for His glory. Jesus ascended for His glory. The Holy Spirit lives in all Christians for His glory. Jesus will come back again for His glory. In the salvific plan of all mankind, God is to be seen for His glory. The God, three in one, God the Father, God the Son, and God the Spirit, exist in perfect unity as one trinity for His glory.

If a religion’s ultimate aim is not to glorify the God of the Bible, it is no religion at all! It is not about the worshipper, it is about the recipient of worship. Man was created for God, not the other way around. This is the stumbling block of this post-modern age when the unitarian universalists and the subjective relativists are preaching relative truth built on the shifting sands of the human heart. To define truth and to determine for the self what is right and wrong — that is giving glory to the self and not to God. The Bible demands glory to be given only to God.

For the Christian, it is an honor and a joy to give to God what He is owed. It is a privilege for the Christian that he has been regenerated to know God, have faith in Him, and to be saved thereby. What kind of man who has been saved from an eternity of torment to be delivered into an eternity of joy would not want to glorify the one who died and rose again for him?


Conclusion

The Christian is an individual who has been saved by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone, as revealed through the Scriptures alone, and to the glory of God alone. Those who can affirm such a truth are those who believe the traditional doctrines of the orthodox Christian faith. A Christian knows of the fallen nature whence he came from to recognize that he could only be saved by the grace of God that is gifted through the faith in Jesus as the Messiah who fulfilled all the covenants of God to the purposes of glorifying only God — the Christian knows this through the Scriptures.
