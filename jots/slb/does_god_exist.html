Does God exist?

Famously, it is said that Bertrand Russell, a British philosopher, when asked what he would say if he stood before the face of God at his death, exclaimed, "Not enough evidence! Not enough evidence!" And along with this British philosopher screams the rest of the world, "We want proof that God exists!"


However, such a demand is more foolish and insolent than a dog barking out to its owner and sustainer, "I want proof that you exist!" How ridiculous would it be if the dependent dog starts to doubt the existence of the very hand that feeds him and cares for him? If it is ridiculous with regard to the dog to its owner, then it is utterly preposterous with regard to the man to his God. Both dog and man are creatures; however, man and God are utterly different. God is the Creator; whereas man is a creature.


To understand this difference between God and man, we go to Moses's first encounter with God. To Moses, that former Israelite-born prince of Egypt and now wilderness shepherd, God appeared in a burning bush (Ex 3). When Moses came near to this bush, he saw something odd about the way in which the bush burned. There was a consuming fire around the bush, yet the fire did not consume the bush. The fire, unlike any other fire which requires a fuel source to exist, was existing upon its own power. And out of this fire God speaks. He tells Moses his name, what is called the "personal name of God." He says, "I AM WHO I AM" (Ex 3:14).


What does this mean? What does it mean that God is who he is?


Well, the LORD our God is the God who is beyond all things. if the whole world were not to exist, he would still be who he is. Unlike us who are in need of other things, who are necessarily in relation to other things, God is not in need of anything and he does not need to be in relation to anything (Acts 17:24-5). He has all that he needs in himself. Therefore, he can only speak of himself in his eternal self as, "I AM WHO I AM." It's not even that God defines himself, but that he is all that defines him.


However, in the case of the creature, in the case of man, we cannot answer in such a way. When some asks, "Who are you?", we answer, "I am a man," or "I am a student," or "I am a writer," et cetera. In other words, you define yourself according to something outside of you. You are a creature in a world in which you relate to and act with respect to other things which gives you your identity.


Above all these creaturely relations, however, you live in relation to God who created you as the Creator. The God who is beyond and above all things, the I AM, has defined who you are since the beginning of creation (Gen 1:26-2:25; Ps 8). Not only in creation, but God continues to sustain and to give life to all that exists in the world (John 1:3; Acts 17:28). Furthermore, it is only in the triune God of the Bible, three persons, one essence (more on this later), that we can distinguish ourselves from other creatures and relate with one another. God both has created all things, and keeps all things in motion.


In other words, you exist because God is who he is. Only because God is the "I AM WHO I AM," you can say, "I am...".


If so, the existence of God is not a matter to be questioned, but a "presupposition" that all hold -- something that is assumed from the outset of any and all thought or inquiry. It's not that you exist and therefore must now prove God, but that because God exists, you can even begin to have thoughts or questions about anything. Even the fact that the unbeliever can scream out, "Give me proof!" is grounded in the fact that God exists.


The Bible tells us that everyone knows God-- that the triune God of the Bible exists, and that they are without excuse (Rom 1:20). All of the heavens, the earth, and even their very selves testify to all men that God exists. However, these same people, in sinfulness, cover up the truth and fool themselves (Rom 1:21-2). They scream at the top of their lungs that God does not exist. And it is a ridiculous sight. It's the sight of dogs denying the existence of the hand that feeds them daily. It's the sight of the bed bug denying the existence of the bed in which it lives. It's the sight of an insane man covering treasure with his own defecation and asking where the treasure is.


To these people who screech for evidence, we point to the whole world-- including themselves-- as their evidence. We show that none of these things exist save for the "I AM WHO I AM." Without this God that they have pretended not to know by burying their own minds in foolishness and filth, nothing in the world makes sense. Therefore, even as they deny him, they search for God. As Augustine, the great theologian of the 4th century said, "Our hearts are restless until they find their rest in you." All know that they are nothing without God. In fact, there is no culture, no people in the world who do not believe in a higher power. Even the so-called atheistic scientists believe in an almost divine meaning of science that rules all things-- somehow science and their own logic is the great thing that defines all things.


You see, when the world asks, "Where is the proof?" Our response is, "How do you make sense of the world without God?" How do you claim to exist if there is no God? How do you know what you know without God? How do you claim to have morality if there is no God? How do you know the world will not suddenly crumble into dust the next second without God's promise that it will not? How do you know what is the meaning of your life without God? These questions require us to communicate with those who ask for proof. It requires us to have a relationship with them. But most importantly, these questions require that we know God ourselves and that we can answer those same questions with the "if there is no God" conditional removed. We need to know who God is.
