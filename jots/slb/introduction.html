Introduction

There is a bird called the common cuckoo. In many ways, it's just another bird. It is, as the name suggests, relatively common, and grows to be a medium sized bird of around a foot in length. It lives in Asia and Europe in the summers and migrates to Africa for the winters.


However, there is one thing that is particularly peculiar about this species of bird. It is, what is commonly called, a "brood parasite." It's a parasite that exploits other birds to raise its brood, its young. When the time comes, the common cuckoo doesn't go about the typical duty of other birds to gather the hay and sticks for the construction of a nest. Rather, it flies about looking for the nests of other birds. And once it has determined its target, a nest full of other eggs, it lays its own offspring and flies away.


What follows is devastating for the victim. Though there are times the mother of the nest is able to dispose of the strange egg in the midst, more often than not, the offending egg remains hidden amongst its new "siblings." If the cuckoo hatches prior to the native inhabitants of the nest, it will methodologically proceed to the disposal of its "siblings." One by one, the eggs are rolled out of the nest onto the ground beneath. The fate of those who hatch prior to the cuckoo are not very optimistic either. The cuckoo, being around three times bigger than the average reed warbler young (a common victim), easily pushes the other hatchlings out of the nest or simply takes up all the attention of the "parents." Soon, only the cuckoo remains in the nest, and the cuckoo soon dwarfs the parents that feed it.


The scene is certainly surprising, if not horrific. Imagine raising your children in your home only to see a monster slowly dispose of your own kids, soon to overpower even you. It is a frightening sight. And to make matters worse, what is most awful about the whole ordeal is that the parents of the nest fail to recognize their mistake as the cuckoo grows. You have become accustomed to the monster.


How does this matter to us?


Well, the picture of this parasite, the cuckoo, is a picture of how the lies that permeate the culture around us incessantly make their breach into our minds. The world around us is full of ideas and thoughts. In a world dominated by information and filled with all kinds of contraptions that feed us that information, we are bound to find a cuckoo's egg in our mind's nests. Our social media feeds and even schools and friends tell us about "ways to live," and about "ways to think." And though we may dismiss some of these ideas and even laugh at some of them, they often surreptitiously take root. In a blink of an eye, in a swipe or double tap of the finger, an egg rests in our mind's nest.


How many times have you found yourself laughing at or ridiculing a certain post you saw on social media only to start sympathizing with it after enough exposure? When you get a chance, try thinking back at the kinds of content you have been scrolling through and the kinds of thoughts you have had recently. You will find that there is a remarkable similarity. We naturally imitate the things around us.


Right now, these thoughts might seem ridiculous, or even just harmless, yet they soon show their true colors. The egg hatches, and this idea, this thought, looks different from the others. It's a little monstrous looking. It's a little more violent. It's a little more indulgent.


Then, very soon, it starts to roll away the true eggs. It takes the place of truth and deposits lies into your mind. One by one, the truth you knew seems to disappear and lies seem to get larger and larger. Of course, as this happens, like the parents of the nest, you might become more and more blind to reality. You might start to confuse lies as truth, truth as lies. You have soon forgotten your own eggs; you have rejected your sound mind. Though the monsters stare back at you, you fail to see its devouring jaws.


What then can we do? What are we to do in a world that screams at us lies from every direction?


There are two steps we must take.


First, we need to recognize the difference between what is true and what is false. To do this, it is impossible for us to catalog all the false things. No, instead, we study what is true and keep clear in our heads what is the truth so that when the lie rolls up, we know that it doesn't fit the bill. We create mental locks in our heads that only truth can open. All other keys that do not fit are lies to be thrown away. The true eggs are kept safe; whereas the false eggs are disposed of quickly.


Second, we need to take care of the eggs and nurture them to maturity. It is not sufficient for the nest to be cleansed of false eggs regularly. Our minds are massive nests, and we will miss the false eggs from time to time. Therefore, we must quickly hatch the true eggs and feed them so that they might be able to defend against the cuckoo hatchlings. The true ideas and thoughts in our heads must develop their own talons and beaks to attack and defend our minds.


Both of these steps are the goals of this study. First, to distinguish, we need to know what is true-- what God tells us is true in his Holy Word. We need to study the Bible and what it says to us about God and about us and our salvation. Second, to develop and mature, we need to go deeper with these truths to give them weapons against the enemy-- so that we might give an answer when the lies attack us. We need to take the contents of the Bible and make it truly a part of our lives. It needs to become a part of who we are so that we might not only be able to distinguish with our minds, but grow in our whole lives to look more and more like what we are supposed to be as Christians.


There is only one answer to the constant flood of ideas and thoughts that inundate our minds. We need to take the tar and pitch of the preserving Word of our God to waterproof our minds from the deathly waters that come in. And just as the preservation of our lives in a flood requires intentionality, so we must apply the same to the reading of Scripture. We must read our Bibles intentionally.


If you are being intentional in your reading, when you read a sentence like, "John always took an umbrella wherever he went," you wouldn't stop at the rote fact of his behavior. You will delve deeper to understand why. You will say, "John is a careful man." Furthermore, as new sentences are written about John, you would add those new characteristics about him to his overall identity. We explore the breadth of who John is along with his depth.


We must do the same thing when we read Scripture. It tells us, through stories, poetry, and letters, about who God is and who we are. We must read the Bible with the intention of learning more about God, about us, and about what duties God requires of us. And because this is a matter of both reflecting deeply and reading widely in the whole of the Bible, it will take time. It will require us to think and to categorize things. It will require us to be diligent in getting to know the God who is the protagonist of the whole Bible.


Our hope in this study is that in reading the Bible intentionally and learning what the whole Bible has to say, we will be able to develop the eyes we need to see the world biblically.
