Emotions

An understanding of what an emotion (or a feeling) is is necessary.


By emotion or feeling, I do not mean just a physical response to something — that is, a physical stimulation. Rather, emotion or feeling, I seek to define as the stirring within the soul or the soul's response to some information that occurs to the individual. To reiterate, emotion is a response, a stimulation within the heart to a certain processing within the mind of the individual to an information perceived by the individual. This immediate response is within the individual's soul, but as we are not only the soul, but also the body, what occurs in the soul naturally affects the body. This, we understand as the physical emotions that we feel and understand. For the Christian, the body and the soul are together one being, and therefore one affects the other. This means that the body affects the soul, and the soul affects the body.

So, what exactly is an emotion the response to? As we discussed above, we see that the body and the soul affect each other. What this means is that although the emotion in itself springs from the soul and outward to the body, the body as well as the soul can be the point of an emotion's cause — the body can influence the soul in a way to affect change to the emotion of an individual. Perhaps we can call this the influence of physical information that the body perceives, and the soul (and specifically the mind) interprets to a certain emotion (to our hearts). This physical information can be something as simple as seeing the color red or as complex as witnessing the death of another, yet this physical information works through the mind to direct the heart. Of course, information or influence upon the soul directly can affect the mind to direct the heart as well (such as God Himself). Just to be clear here, I do not mean to divide the soul into distinct metaphysical parts of mind and heart, but to distinguish the separate functions the soul accomplishes into departments of the mind and the heart. Rather, the mind can only process something a certain way because the heart directs it so, and the heart can only feel a certain way because the mind affects it so. The two are simultaneously affecting each other because outside the abstractions, they are but one soul.

To clarify and to summarize, I would like to say that an emotion or a feeling is a stirring within and by the heart to the mind's analysis (or understanding) of a given information, whether that information be within the spiritual realm or from the body.

And it makes sense for us Christians that the emotion be a response rather than the cause as we see throughout the Bible instructions for the soul to feel a certain way. For one, we hear the psalmists say, "rejoice," over and over again throughout the psalms as if we can control our emotions because perhap, we are built to. If emotion is a response to an understanding of something within our minds and the alignment of our hearts toward something, then that understanding and alignment must bring about a certain type of stirring within us. What changes foremost to bring about the response is the cause, not the response.

And therefore, we can make the statement that no emotion apart from the expresser of the said emotion is in itself good or bad. Rather, an emotion is good or bad based on whether or not it is justified by the mind's reason and the heart's affinity working in union. That is to say, what the heart produces is justified by what the mind elicits in the heart. We can say that we have an emotion towards something that we have derived our emotion from. More specifically, the soul of the individual and its inclination thereof determines whether or not the emotion is good or bad. To take it a step further, no emotion in itself exists in isolation. As emotion is a response, it is the response to the reason and desire of the soul.

Yet, we know that all who do not know God are dead, and as a result their souls are but a desolate desert that produces evil thoughts and reason within the mind and a deceitful heart bent towards evil, and therefore the soul can only produce unjustified emotions that are evil as well. In our sin nature, as we have claimed and put ourselves as the center of our own standard for reason, and have placed ourselves as gods in our hearts, we have an inherently flawed reasoning that as a result cannot but produce emotion that is incorrect in its direction of inclination as well. We had originally been created to set God as our standard of all reason and praise, and naturally, this effectively meant that our emotions were accurately guided as well, but now, through the fall of man, our emotions are but responses to misguided logic that are directed towards the wrong god.

Though God had originally created all human emotion to be in the likeness of Himself — that is, centered around the understanding of and worship to Himself, we, by eating from the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, have chosen autonomy to create our own flawed understandings centered around ourselves that produce flawed emotions as well.

However still, there is good news, the Good News. This Good News is that Jesus, God Himself incarnate, came to earth, His creation, to die for His creation, mankind, so that we may be redeemed to Himself and be reborn in His image as He has resurrected. To be reborn, we need only to have faith in Jesus Christ and in doing so, we are completely new creations. We are reborn with Christ in God. As a result, not only do we have a new trajectory and hope for our lives as we await our own bodily death and resurrection to a new body, but we already have a new soul that can place our understanding and all reasoning once again upon God. Not only that, but the regenerated heart directs the worship, not towards idols, but towards God. As a result, we can have emotions that are truly good.

Furthermore, by our understanding of God and our emotions that result, we can come to understand God furthermore. Though emotion is a response, we often take that response from the heart as a variable to understand and reason within the mind that once again directs the heart which in turn facilitates the mind. For the non-Christian, this would be a self-feeding loop, yet for the Christian, since all understanding is from and guided by God, the response that is our emotion feeds into our understanding which then is shaped and guided by the Spirit in us (who is God Himself) to work in our renewed minds and hearts once again to God. We can love the Lord our God with all our hearts, our souls, and minds because within us is no longer us who live, but Christ who lives in us, the Logos Himself.

This idea that emotion is a response to reason and understanding is important because it also helps us to know how the Holy Spirit works within us. We see that throughout the Bible God reveals Himself — there are revelations of God. Because emotion is a response, God does not inject an emotion for us to feel, but God reveals Himself, and we are able to feel accordingly if we have hearts of flesh. Love, for example, we understand not as an emotion first, but actually through an understanding of what has been shown to us through the work of God throughout all of history, and most importantly through the work on the cross. Such an understanding can only be possible within the mind of one who has been reborn already with a heart that dedicates that understanding towards the right recipient of our worship. As a result of understanding the love of Christ throughout both the Old and the New Testament, we can finally have the response of love in emotion that stirs within our hearts of flesh. This we can then in the soul comprehend and feel, and in the body, act out. We can only love because we have been loved first.

Yet, of course, we are not only the soul but also the body -- meaning our redemption is "already but not yet". However, this is not a deterrence, but a process -- our souls have been reborn already, and we are in the "not yet," waiting for the resurrection of our bodies. Though we have been reborn in the Spirit, we often still look to our old selves and follow the folly of our earlier days. We often listen to our old, evil logos instead of the new and stoke up our old, idolatrous hearts toward ourselves and put the resulting emotion as our guideline -- which is emotionalism. Other times, we often disconnect and ignore the responses from our hearts to become stoics. Sometimes, our minds may try to find a good reason to point the heart to squeeze out a response, but fail to do so when our hearts seem to be hardened. Yet further still, our dead bodies often do not reflect the new, living souls that we have within us and are broken to produce runaway physical stimulation that does not originate from within the soul (such as clinical depression). Other times, we cannot properly express the beautiful expressions of our emotions from the inside to the outside.

From the reality of living with such fallen nature of ours, we may dismay and lose hope. Yet if we do so, such a conclusion and feeling of desperation we arrive at would be falling victim to our old selves. We do not dwell on our failures because we have a guarantee whose name is Jesus. Though we are now at war with ourselves and sometimes lament of why we do what we do not want to do, we have a redeeming hope because of Christ who is our new Logos. Even when we recognize our fallen selves, we look once again to our Champion who went outside the camp for us, and ran the race set before him. In looking to both our prize and champion, we can be joyful once again.

Furthermore, through all this, in this body, we come to know how much the cost was, and we have an opportunity to share how great the new life is to those who are still dead and cannot truly feel. Among lepers who cannot feel, we live as healed people who have felt the most amazing feeling of His love, and we can share that good love. If this is a true parable, we understand that those who are healed shall no more live like lepers, and put off the old self of rotting skin and dead flesh. We, those reborn, do so by the grace of God in works of remembering of, learning of, and communing with God, the three in one.

Emotions, thereby, are real, effectual responses of our hearts to the reasoning of our minds and alignment of our hearts. And if they are a response within our "already but not yet" souls of deceitful hearts guided by broken minds, we do not set them alone as our center of attention in living our lives. Rather, we focus on God the Redeemer to sanctify us, to turn our hearts to the Father’s heart, and to reason us through His Logos of how we should feel. For it is no longer we who live, but Christ who lives in us.

An Essay by a man who struggles daily with his own emotions April 22, 2020 - Handa Chun
